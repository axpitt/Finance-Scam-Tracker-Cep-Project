# Antigravity Website Build Brief

## Project

**Scam or Smart Deal? Investment Hype Awareness**  
Community Engagement Project (CEP), MIT Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar  
SY B.Tech, Academic Year 2026–27

## Instructions for Antigravity

Build a complete, polished, responsive, accessible awareness website from this brief. Implement the working website, not just a mockup. Treat the rest of this document as the complete product and content specification. Use sensible defaults, do not stop to ask questions, and keep the code simple enough for a student to understand and edit.

The finished site must be **static and self-contained**. Do not add a backend, login, database, analytics, external form, payment feature, market-data feed, stock-price lookup, live news, or any other live data. All scenarios, posts, profiles, amounts, and quiz content must be fictional examples written into the site. Quiz answers stay in browser memory and disappear when the page is reloaded.

Do not invent student names, roll numbers, guide names, survey findings, participant counts, testimonials, impact, evaluation results, or deployment status. Use editable placeholders for team details. Do not copy any other website’s text, names, graphics, screenshots, or branding. Do not give personalized investment advice or tell visitors which asset to buy or sell.

## 1. Purpose and audience

Help college students and community members recognize warning signs in online investment promotions, especially social-media stock tips, fake trading apps, and promises of quick or guaranteed profits. Use plain English, short explanations, and practical next steps. Assume visitors may have little knowledge of investing or financial terminology.

This is a community education project, not a trading platform, investment service, financial adviser, broker, regulator, bank, or cybercrime reporting service. It must not simulate an actual investment or ask visitors to provide money or personal information.

## 2. Learning outcomes

After using the site, a visitor should be able to:

1. Identify common warning signs in online investment promotions, including guaranteed or unusually high returns, urgency, unverifiable profit screenshots, unfamiliar app links, and blocked withdrawals.
2. Understand that every securities-market investment carries risk and past results or displayed profits do not guarantee future results.
3. Pause before acting and verify the identity of an entity or platform through independently located official sources.
4. Understand that checking registration is one verification step; it does not guarantee profit or make every message, account, or offer safe.
5. Know that suspected cyber financial fraud can be reported through India’s official cybercrime channels and that a report does not guarantee recovery.

## 3. Site structure

Create a single-page website with clear navigation and these sections:

- **Home** — project purpose, a strong but calm opening message, and buttons to start the challenge or learn the warning signs.
- **Red Flags** — concise cards explaining the main warning signs in investment promotions.
- **Scam Spotter Challenge** — interactive fictional social-media posts. Visitors choose a response and receive an explanation.
- **How the Fake App Pattern Works** — a simple visual sequence based on the official SEBI advisory.
- **Quick Quiz** — an interactive knowledge check with immediate explanations, score summary, and retry button.
- **If Money Was Sent** — calm, actionable next steps and official reporting links.
- **About This CEP** — project purpose and editable team/guide placeholders. Do not claim the project was tested, surveyed, deployed, or evaluated.
- **Sources** — named official references with direct links and a checked date.

Use a persistent but unobtrusive navigation bar, a clear mobile menu, a footer with source/reporting links, and a back-to-top button for long pages.

## 4. Verified educational content

Use the following points accurately and in plain language:

- SEBI warns visitors to be suspicious of assured, guaranteed, near-certain, or unusually high returns. Securities-market investments carry risk.
- Be cautious of entities or promoters whose relevant regulatory status cannot be verified. Check through official SEBI or exchange resources, reached independently.
- High-pressure messages such as “act now,” “limited seats,” or “don’t miss out” are warning signs. Do not make a rushed decision because a post creates urgency.
- Be wary of complicated strategies that the promoter cannot explain clearly, missing documentation, and success stories or screenshots offered as proof without independent verification.
- Fake trading-app scams may begin with social-media ads or messaging groups, build trust through supposed experts and screenshots, introduce an unfamiliar app, display fake profits, pressure users to add more money, and block withdrawals or demand extra fees.
- Unverified app links or APK files, payments to third-party accounts, and guaranteed securities-market returns are red flags. Encourage visitors to check app/platform legitimacy using official exchange or SEBI investor resources.
- A warning sign is a reason to pause and verify; the website must not claim that one sign alone proves a particular person or company committed a crime.
- Past performance, popularity, followers, testimonials, screenshots, and a professional-looking app do not by themselves verify an offer or guarantee future returns.
- Do not imply that a SEBI registration check guarantees safety, profit, or that a social-media account contacting the visitor belongs to the registered entity.

## 5. Red-flag cards

Include at least these six cards. Each card should have a short title, a plain-language explanation, and a “What to do” step.

1. **Guaranteed or unusually high returns** — A promise of fixed or certain profits in securities-market trading is a strong warning sign. Pause and check the claim through credible official information.
2. **Pressure to act quickly** — “Today only,” “limited seats,” and “last chance” language tries to rush a decision. Take time to verify independently.
3. **Unverified investment guru or private tip group** — A large following, paid group, or confident expert persona is not proof of registration, expertise, or results. Check the exact entity and service independently.
4. **Profit screenshots as proof** — Screenshots can be incomplete or fabricated and do not prove the whole track record. Do not rely on them as verification.
5. **Unfamiliar app link or APK** — Do not install an app sent through an unsolicited message. Verify the platform using official exchange or SEBI resources.
6. **More money required to withdraw** — A displayed balance followed by blocked withdrawals, new fees, or pressure to borrow is a serious warning sign. Do not send additional money just because the app displays profits.

## 6. Interactive scenarios

Create at least four fictional social-media or messaging examples. Use made-up names and values only. Do not use real phone numbers, real account details, UPI IDs, real company accusations, or scannable QR codes. Each example must offer two or three choices, then explain the warning signs and a safer next step.

1. **Guaranteed daily profit post** — “Earn fixed daily returns from stock trading. No risk. Join before midnight.” Correct learning point: guaranteed returns, no-risk language, and urgency are warning signs; pause and verify.
2. **Private stock-tips group** — A fictional creator shows selected profit screenshots and charges a fee for “exclusive calls.” Correct learning point: screenshots and exclusivity do not verify results or registration; check the source independently and do not rush.
3. **Trading app download link** — A group admin sends an unfamiliar APK link and asks members to deposit to an unrelated account. Correct learning point: unverified app links and third-party payment requests are red flags; do not install or pay based on the message.
4. **Withdrawal fee demand** — A fictional app shows a large profit but says a new fee must be paid before withdrawal. Correct learning point: blocked withdrawals and extra fee demands match warning patterns in SEBI’s fake trading-app advisory; do not send more money based on the displayed balance.

Do not make the interaction depend on colour alone. After every answer, show a clear text label such as “Warning signs found” and explain why.

## 7. Quiz

Build at least six multiple-choice questions based on the content above. Give immediate, supportive feedback for correct and incorrect choices. At the end, show the score, a short learning summary, and a retry button. State that the quiz is only a learning activity and is not a financial-competence test.

Include questions covering:

- Guaranteed returns in securities-market promotions.
- Why urgency should make someone pause.
- Why profit screenshots do not verify an offer.
- What to do with an unfamiliar app/APK link.
- What to make of blocked withdrawals and extra fee demands.
- Where to report suspected cyber financial fraud in India.

Keep quiz answers only in page memory. Do not store, transmit, or log quiz responses.

## 8. If money was sent

Present calm general information, not a guarantee of recovery:

1. Contact the bank or payment provider through its official channel as soon as possible and ask what steps apply to the transaction.
2. For suspected cyber financial fraud in India, call **1930** promptly or use the [National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal](https://www.cybercrime.gov.in/).
3. Preserve relevant messages, transaction references, dates, and complaint numbers for official follow-up. Do not post sensitive evidence publicly.
4. Do not pay anyone who promises guaranteed recovery of lost money.

Explain that 1930 and the portal are official reporting routes for cyber financial fraud; they are not a promise that money will be recovered and are not a substitute for bank, police, SEBI, or exchange instructions. For securities-market complaints, link to the official [SEBI SCORES portal](https://scores.sebi.gov.in/) and tell users to follow the relevant official complaint guidance.

## 9. Visual direction

Create an original, modern editorial visual identity for an investor-awareness education site. Use a warm off-white or pale background, deep navy text, restrained teal for safe actions, and amber for caution. Use red sparingly for urgent warnings. Build a strong headline, readable cards, clear typographic hierarchy, generous spacing, and small CSS-based illustrations or icons. Do not use bank, broker, exchange, or SEBI logos as decorative branding. Do not copy another site’s graphics.

Make the site feel engaging and student-friendly, not like a trading dashboard or commercial fintech product. Do not show live prices, charts, market tickers, investment calculators, current stock recommendations, or simulated profit dashboards. Use color, icons, and text labels together so meaning is never communicated by color alone.

## 10. Accessibility and usability

- Use semantic HTML landmarks, a logical heading order, real buttons and links, and descriptive accessible names.
- Ensure all navigation and scenario/quiz interactions work with keyboard alone.
- Provide a visible focus indicator and sufficient text/background contrast.
- Make the mobile menu keyboard-operable and expose its open/closed state.
- Announce scenario and quiz feedback accessibly, such as with an appropriate live region.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid flashing or distracting animation.
- Use readable font sizes and responsive layouts for phones, tablets, and desktop.
- Explain unfamiliar terms in plain language the first time they appear.

## 11. Privacy and security boundaries

- Build a static, front-end-only site. Do not create an API, backend, account system, database, external form, analytics, trackers, or third-party chat widget.
- Do not request or collect names, phone numbers, email addresses, government IDs, bank details, investment account details, UPI IDs, passwords, PINs, OTPs, screenshots, or personal loss stories.
- Do not send quiz choices or other visitor activity anywhere. Keep temporary quiz state in memory and clear it on reload.
- Do not embed live market data, live news, external financial widgets, or remote content feeds. All website content should work offline after the files are loaded, except that official reference links naturally require internet access when opened.
- Open external source links safely in a new tab and use HTTPS.

## 12. Technical expectations

First inspect the available project files and preserve the existing setup. If the workspace is empty, create a simple static site with `index.html`, `css/styles.css`, `js/app.js`, and a `README.md`. Avoid frameworks and dependencies unless the workspace already requires them. Keep the copy easy to edit and the interactions understandable for a student.

The README should explain how to open/run the website, where to update educational text and fictional scenarios, and how to replace the student/guide placeholders. Include a short checklist for checking official links, keyboard use, mobile layout, and content before presenting the website. Do not state that it has been deployed or tested with the community unless that actually happens.

- The site is fully static and contains no backend, live data, analytics, or visitor-data submission.
- Navigation links reach the correct sections and the mobile menu works.
- All scenario choices reveal accurate, understandable explanations.
- Quiz answers, feedback, score, and retry flow work.
- The website remains usable on a narrow mobile screen and by keyboard alone.
- No fictional scenario is presented as a real person, company, or confirmed case.
- The content distinguishes warning signs from proof and does not promise recovery or investment returns.
- Official sources are clearly named and linked.
- Student and guide fields remain editable placeholders.
- No invented survey statistics, testimonials, participants, deployment, or impact claims appear.
- README instructions are sufficient for a student to run and edit the site.
